Reviewed September 26, 2026. Status: verified locally for the Acne module. This is an agent-performed review, not independent pharmacist review or certification.

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed the actual nine lessons, nine teaching diagrams, nine embedded checks and all 119 assessment stems, choices, keys and explanations. Reconciled RxPrep 2023 chapter 39 acne material (printed pages 547-549), including its topical and oral treatment tables, with the 2024 AAD guideline, current FDA/DailyMed labeling, FDA/iPLEDGE updates, CDC contraceptive eligibility guidance and NICE treatment guidance. The chronological source-by-source record is `ch39-initial-review.json`; its `acneCompletion` entry supersedes earlier pending statements for this module only.

- Corrected Azelex 20% cream versus Finacea 15% gel and their labeled indications; separated Duobrii psoriasis treatment from acne retinoids.
- Distinguished dapsone formulations, age thresholds, application schedules, blood-related warnings, G6PD risk and lactation counseling.
- Added exact retinoid instructions, combination-product preparation and storage, topical minocycline handling, clascoterone storage and systemic-risk interpretation. Replaced universal pea-sized dosing in lesson, diagram and assessment with product-specific amounts.
- Expanded oral antibiotic dosing, administration, stewardship, interaction and reproductive counseling. Explained the limited role and evidence for alternative antibiotics.
- Expanded combined-contraceptive eligibility, spironolactone contraindications, interactions and individualized potassium monitoring.
- Expanded isotretinoin formulation-specific dose/food instructions, whole-capsule handling, independently checked calculations, contraception selection, dispensing authorization, laboratory monitoring and symptom action plans.
- Replaced ambiguous assessment choices with focused cases and expanded the bank to 119 explicit, reviewable questions. Retained accurate existing content.

## Important source differences

RxPrep transposes azelaic-acid strengths, places Duobrii in its acne table, generalizes topical dapsone G6PD avoidance, and gives isotretinoin directions that do not fit every current product. Current labels control product-specific details. Absorica/LD breastfeeding intervals differ from the book's one-month blanket statement; pregnancy prevention and blood donation retain their separate one-month rules. Capsules are kept whole rather than punctured into food.

The original AAD text contains an Epiduo strength and a dapsone age statement that conflict with current product labels. Those facts were resolved independently from labeling. The exact 2026 erratum was inaccessible and is not claimed as read. CDC broad-spectrum-antibiotic contraceptive guidance and AAD healthy-patient spironolactone monitoring guidance are distinguished explicitly from broader label warnings. Current iPLEDGE implementation dates, rather than historical memorized steps or approval dates, control dispensing.
